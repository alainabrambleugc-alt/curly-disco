Creator Profile — Alaina Bramble (Mama WildRoot Creative)
Paste-ready rules for the brief teardown engine. These rules override generic UGC advice everywhere. When a brief conflicts with anything here, flag the conflict — don't smooth it over.
1. Niche and identity

* UGC creator, mid-40s, perimenopausal, mom of a homeschooled third-grader, filming from an off-grid homestead (a Quonset hut) outside Hilo, Hawai'i. Business name: Mama WildRoot Creative.
* Casting lanes: 40-something mom; Hawai'i / off-grid / homestead life; real skin (rosacea); perimenopause and women's wellness; ADHD (public about her ADHD systems); sleep — lifelong insomnia, cannot use melatonin (it leaves her groggy), angle is "it's 2 AM and you're awake again"; tech and Canvas app campaigns in bulk short-form; family and homeschool life.
* Background that shapes her delivery: 20 years of face-to-face sales, journalism and public speaking, a degree in marketing psychology. She translates sales psychology to social media; she does not position herself as a beginner at selling.
* Her audience: women roughly 35–55, moms, neurodivergent women. Her personal-brand audience is a 40-something, probably neurodivergent woman, off-grid or heading there, homeschooling.
* Real record (the only proof numbers allowed): about 6 months in UGC, ~$15,000 made, ~$7,000 in one month, ~$2,000/month since, on 10–20 hours a week. Signed directly with CapCut for its Canvas campaign; a Darwin video of hers runs as a paid brand ad.

2. On-camera style

* She always films from a teleprompter script. Even when a brief says "natural" or "unscripted," she plans the shots and talking points and scripts it — flag any brief that demands true improvisation as a workflow conflict, not a dealbreaker.
* Before filming, she sends the brand a storyboard for approval — shot list and talking points — on every project. A teardown should assume this step happens; briefs with no approval step are fine, she adds hers.
* Story-first, always: a lived moment opens (a personal, specific pain point or confession), and the product enters as the answer. Never open with the product's name and functions.
* First 3 seconds carry a triple hook: visual + verbal + on-screen text. Structure after the hook: payoff tease (3–8s), proof with a new shot every 3–5 seconds, midpoint re-hook, payoff, exactly one CTA.
* Warm, candid, specific, concrete numbers from real life. Humor is real-life and creator-life (rates, revisions, filming chaos at home), self-aware, never polished-influencer.
* Real locations and natural light preferred; polish lives in framing and edit, not in persona. She wears a neck fan constantly in the Hilo humidity and removes it only while filming.
* Camera defaults: 1080p at 30 fps, HDR off, vertical 9:16, lens wiped, daylight spots, hands busy in frame.

3. Phrases she uses — and words she never uses
Uses: "Mahalo!" (her sign-off in messages). Plain, warm, direct sentences. Concrete specifics — real numbers, real timeframes, real moments. Systems language for ADHD content (loops, stuck points, "a system outside your head"). Abundance and accountability language in personal-brand content: 1% better every day, get back up, keep going, you are loved exactly as you are.
Never:

* Never claims a result, symptom, or story she has not lived — no invented proof, ever. If a teardown needs a proof point that doesn't exist, the slot is [NEED], not a placeholder claim.
* Never states she is an alcoholic, and never implies a personal or family alcohol (or similar) story for a product.
* Never tells a client they are her first inbound brand.
* No apology language in follow-ups, and no invented urgency ("only 2 spots left" unless it's her real, dated special).
* Never frames herself publicly as "learning alongside" her audience.
* No em dashes in Trybe messages (and Trybe notes cap at 1,000 characters).
* Never uses her real last name in business names (liability rule) — the brand is Mama WildRoot Creative.

5. Formats she loves — and formats to flag
Loves: talking head with a triple hook; story-first product demos; B-roll + voiceover (including walk-and-talk); montage demos; one product / five hooks rotations; screen-recording app demos (her Canvas/tech lane); greenscreen explainers; serialized storytelling; pitch videos and brand open calls; faceless caption-led formats when a brief requires them (e.g., Remote Rocket Ship forbids talking head — she adapts, she doesn't decline).
Flag in a teardown:

* Briefs demanding unscripted freestyle delivery (she scripts — see §2).
* Anything requiring her to use, ingest, or apply a product she hasn't tested under a paid project with a signed contract first.
* Supplement/health briefs requiring insomnia or treatment claims, prescription comparisons, specific-results promises, or before/after framing — she won't say them, and most platforms ban them anyway.
* Any sleep app, on any account, while her Morphi agreement runs (exclusivity).
* Briefs requiring her to link accounts through Meta Accounts Center, hand over passwords, or delete/edit posted videos after the fact without agreement.

6. Hard nos (auto-flag any brief containing these)

1. Gifted-only or "exposure" work, and trial/test videos without a paid project secured and a signed contract first. Paid projects only.
2. Products or claims she has not lived and cannot stand behind (integrity rule) — including any script that assigns her a false personal history.
3. Paid usage bundled in free, usage in perpetuity, or whitelisting treated as paid media rights. Whitelisting and paid usage are different lines, always priced separately.
4. The $30 bulk rate applied to anything that isn't Canvas/tech bulk work (typically 10+ videos).
5. Rates below her floor (see §7) presented as take-it-or-leave-it.

7. Rates and terms (for pricing checks in a teardown)

* Standard UGC: starts at $100 for a 15-second video, rising with length. A direct inbound video typically starts at a $200 base.
* Canvas/tech bulk only (typically more than 10 super-short videos): $30/video — includes whitelisting, does not include paid usage on the brand's website.
* Paid usage: priced per 30 days, 3-month minimum, longer packages cheaper per month. Usage is always a separate line from production.
* Any discount is framed as a limited seasonal special, never a beginner price.
* Standard turnaround: delivery 3–5 days after the product is in hand, with the storyboard sent for approval before filming.
* AI never quotes a rate she did not supply for that specific negotiation.
