# Storytelling Guide — Alaina Bramble (Mama WildRoot Creative)

Compiled 2026-11-15 by Rumi from Alaina's own system documents: the Script Engine, the Engagement Standard, the THIS/THAT Hook Skill, the Sheila Script Writer (9 brains × 5 doors), the Julia Conversion Psychology (Fogg) layer, and her Creator Profile. Nothing here is invented — where her documents disagreed, Alaina ruled on each one (2026-11-15); her rulings are written into the sections below and logged at the end. **Where any companion skill or source document disagrees with this guide, this guide wins.**

This guide is read BEFORE any edit or script pass. It governs how Alaina tells stories; the glossary governs what her effects mean; the Classroom governs what her edits should look like.

## 1. MY SPINE — the structure of every video

The Script Engine five-beat spine, with the Engagement Standard's beats bolted in (the Standard supplies the emotional sequence; the Engine supplies structure and pacing). **This is the only spine.** The Creator Intelligence Brief's Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA is retired — do not use it for scripts, captions, pitches, or outreach.

- **0–3s HOOK** — one of the seven hooks in §2. Visual hook too: movement, product in hand, or on-screen text that contradicts the spoken line.
- **3–8s SHOULDER TAP + PROMISE** — the beat right after the hook that makes the viewer stay, not just stop (Lana's shoulder tap, from journalism's nut graf): name who this is for — by identity, by problem, or by specific situation — and tease the payoff without delivering it ("and the third thing is what actually sold me"). Optional layers: timing/urgency, the cost of inaction, or a mistake she's making. **ONE beat doing both jobs** — who it's for AND the payoff tease, in one breath (e.g. "If you're a mom running a business from your phone — the third thing is what fixed it."). Not two separate beats. The Engagement Standard's "payoff tease" checkpoint is this same beat.
- **~25% VALIDATION BEFORE THE WIN** — before any proof, affirm what the audience is already good at and what their experience is worth. Specific, true, earned — never generic flattery. The audience is positioned as experts missing one skill, not beginners starting from zero.
- **MIDDLE PROOF** — show, don't describe. Demo beat, result, number. Cut when a new thought or new proof arrives — never just for motion: a meaningful visual change on every new thought, a new information beat every 7–10 seconds, no static frame longer than 6 seconds. Aim for a fresh shot every 3–5 seconds through proof ONLY where each shot shows something new — no noise cuts (her ruling, 2026-11-16). Demonstration never stops: product in hand, being used, through every line (Sheila's mechanic).
- **50–60% MIDPOINT RE-HOOK + SPECIFIC PROOF** — "but here's what nobody tells you…" A second open loop right where viewers usually drop, paid off immediately with ONE specific, surprising, believable-scale proof point. Specific and small enough to feel reachable — that combination is the point.
- **OBJECTION KILL** — immediately after the proof, one line removes the single biggest dismissal the audience will use ("you'd have to be an influencer"). Name the exact objection; a kill line that answers a different objection fails.
- **END PAYOFF, THEN ONE CTA** — (on sales scripts the payoff lands early instead, by ~20s, and this beat is the last proof before the CTA — see §3 Julia/Fogg). Deliver the teased payoff FIRST (a broken payoff = no trust, no click), then exactly one CTA while the payoff/product is on screen. **CTA type is set by the account on the project card:** brand-attraction and personal-brand accounts → SOFT CTA ("follow along, I'll keep sharing what's working"); brand deliverables → DIRECT CTA that names exactly what the click gets them — the discount, the trial, the shade match — never a bare "link in bio." Never both. One CTA only: two asks = zero clicks.
- **THE REFRAME runs through the whole script** — the viewer finishes feeling smart for where she is: being behind is reframed as the advantage; stuck means a missing system, never a personal failure. If any line shames the viewer, rewrite it.

Word budget: scripts are 30–60 seconds, roughly 75–150 spoken words unless a brief says otherwise. Short sentences — every line must be sayable on camera in one breath.

## 2. MY HOOKS

**The triple hook, always:** visual + verbal + on-screen text, all in the first 3 seconds. Every hook must work three ways at once — spoken line, on-screen text, and a visual filmable in the first 3 seconds. **Under 15 words — hard limit.** The first three words carry the tension. A longer line gets split: the first sentence is the hook, the rest moves into the 3–8s beat.

**The THIS test (Chelsleigh):** a hook is a THIS (flat, reject it) if it could be said about any product in the category — it names the product, states its basic function, or uses empty praise ("obsessed," "amazing," "game changer," "you need this"). A THAT can only be said about THIS product, in THIS life, on THIS day. No empty words in the first 3 seconds; if a line could fit any product, rewrite it.

**MY SEVEN HOOKS — one merged list** (the Script Engine's 4 patterns + the THIS/THAT 5 levers + the Engagement Standard hook, merged 2026-11-15). Use this one list for writing hooks from scratch AND for rewriting a flat THIS.
1. **Survival Test** — the product endures a duration plus an ordeal. "Lasted [time] through [event]."
2. **Emergency In Progress** — the scene is already happening and the clock is running. "I had [minutes] to fix [problem]." (Engine version: 2–3 short chaos beats, product as rescue, end "How's this?")
3. **Named Pain Killed** — one specific, annoying before-state, gone. "It stopped my [exact annoyance]."
4. **Missing Piece** — after trying or changing other things, this completed it. "The piece I was missing after [number] tries." (Absorbs the Engine's Plot Twist: failed attempts, "and somehow this was the missing piece.")
5. **Outcome Over Feature** — a result someone can picture, never an ingredient or spec list. Show the after, not the formula.
6. **One Thing I Changed** — past pain stated plainly, product NOT named.
7. **Named Audience + Withheld Answer** — name the exact audience + state the crisis/tension + withhold the answer, so the only way to get it is to keep watching.

**Who may open with "I":** hooks 1–6 (the lived-moment hooks) may open in first person ("I had 20 minutes to…"). Hook 7 opens on the audience, not "I". Everything else — captions, pitches, outreach, all other copy — never opens with "I" or any ego-forward framing (Creator Intelligence Brief).

Every hook opens on a lived moment — never on the product's name or functions. A lever is only as good as its proof point: no real timeframe, no Survival Test — pick another lever. Rank candidate hooks with the most specific proof point first.

## 3. MY PSYCHOLOGY

### Sheila — pick the BRAIN first, then ONE DOOR

The brain decides the language (what this buyer trusts, what proof she needs, what register she hears as honest). The door decides the structure (how the product enters). A script for the wrong brain fails even through the right door. **The 40s brain is ALWAYS on — every script is written for the over-40 brain.** Still always ask who the persona/buyer is for the ad: the answer adds the layers stacked on top of the 40s brain (e.g. 40s + Mom + Woman, or 40s + Man), never replaces it. Brains layer (a 42-year-old mom is 40s + Mom + Woman): lead with the sharpest one, let the others season the language.

**The persona questions — ask before any script or edit pass** (the persona gives the details the script is built from; never fill these in by guessing — mark [NEED]):
1. Who is this ad for? (adds the layers on top of the 40s brain)
2. What are her pain points — the specific, lived moments where the problem shows up, in the words she would use?
3. How does the product or solution solve each one?
4. What does she FEEL once it's solved? — the emotional outcome (relief, time back, confidence, calm, being trusted), never the feature that causes it.

Each pain point → its emotional outcome becomes the material for the hook, the shoulder tap, the proof, and the payoff. Features only appear as evidence for an outcome, never as the point.

**Always selling, never sounding like an ad.** Every line moves her toward the purchase, and no line sounds like marketing: no ad voice, no hype, no feature lists, no "you need this." It should sound like a friend telling her what changed — the selling lives in the story, the outcome, and the proof.

**The nine brains:**
1. **Boomer — Certainty.** Proven results, shown again and again. Track record and how long it's been around; remove novelty risk.
2. **Gen Z — Realness.** Raw delivery, no polish, no ad voice — they can smell a script through the phone. (For Alaina: the one brain where the teleprompter has to disappear completely.)
3. **Millennial — Identity.** Who she becomes once she owns it; the future self and the values the purchase signals.
4. **Man — Outcome.** Fewer words. What it does, what it costs. Specs and price up front; story is padding. **Applies ONLY when the persona is a man** — Alaina rarely makes content for men. When it does apply, it overrides the lived-moment opening and the explanation rule (§4) for that video.
5. **Woman — Safety and trust.** Over function. Will it work for me, can I trust you, what happens if it doesn't. Risk removal is the pitch.
6. **Mom — Time.** Money comes back; time doesn't. Every claim translated into minutes saved or a task deleted.
7. **20s — Future self.** An investment in the woman she's building: starter, first real one, leveling up.
8. **30s — Efficiency.** Get it right the first time; sell the end of the search — the last one she has to buy.
9. **40s — Respect.** No fluff, no patronizing, price stated directly. Alaina's core audience AND her casting demographic — always on, in every script.

**The five doors** (one per video; rotate all five across a campaign — Feeling, Fix, Joke, Show, Close. **On sales scripts the Show door is OUT** — rotate Feeling, Fix, Joke, Close; research: curiosity is the weakest sales angle):
1. **The Feeling** — sell the outcome/alibi she gets to claim (rested, put-together, free). Product never announced first; it enters as the reason she gets to feel that way.
2. **The Fix** — name the category's known failure from lived experience; the product enters as the specific fix. Proof shown, not described.
3. **The Joke** — a funny, true confession about the ritual or category; the product rides inside the bit as the sane exception. Stay funny while demonstrating.
4. **The Show** — visible result on screen in the first 3 seconds, product unnamed; hold the name to the end / caption / pinned comment (brief permitting). Curiosity does the selling. **Growth/non-sales videos only** — never on a script meant to sell.
5. **The Close** — direct, confident pitch: what it is, what's in it, why it's worth the price. Specifics like a verdict; confidence is what transfers trust.

Test: if a line could be pasted into another brain's script unchanged, it fits none — rewrite it.

### Julia / Fogg — the conversion layer

Behavior happens when motivation + ability are both high at the same moment. Scrolling wins on sensation (dopamine), so never fight on sensation — build on the other two motivation sources:

- **Belonging** — the viewer must feel, in the first seconds, that this video is for her specifically. Name the exact viewer in the opening line. This is the psychology under the shoulder tap and the identity-first opening.
- **Anticipation** — pick ONE positioning per video and commit: **(a) product as solution** (name the pain she lives with; the product enters as the answer; teach exactly how, with the demo as a SHOT inside the video, never the whole format — educational AND aspirational, both, every time), or **(b) product as the hook** (the product's own experience/interface is the interesting thing and carries the video).
- **The ~20-second payoff rule** — inside roughly the first 20 seconds, sell the outcome and make the promise bigger than her next few scrolls. If the payoff is smaller than the scroll, she scrolls. **How this fits the spine — depends on the video:** on SALES scripts (anything meant to sell), DELIVER the payoff as early as honestly possible (by ~20s) and never withhold it to the end; everything after it is proof, then the CTA. On growth and storytelling videos, PROMISE the outcome clearly by ~20s (in the 3–8s beat or early proof) and DELIVER the full payoff at the end, right before the CTA.
- **Judge by conversion signals, not views** — mobilizing comments ("what product is this?", "how do I download?", "sending this to…") and shares. Educational + aspirational + motivating content gets sent.

**Mandatory 5-point pre-flight on every conversion script:** (1) Belonging — exact viewer named in the opening line? (2) Anticipation — solution or hook, picked and committed? (3) Educational AND aspirational — both present? (4) Payoff in ~20 seconds, bigger than the scroll? (5) Built to convert — made to earn "what product is this?" comments and shares, not just views? If a point fails, fix it or flag the miss out loud — never silently ship it.

### Dawn's seven rules (Dawn Flaherty @builtformoresis, Aug 29, 2026 reel — a SEPARATE source from Julia; also filed in the Pitching Playbook §4d)

Emotion first · pain moves faster than pleasure · sales is clarity · objections are fear · urgency is the cost of staying the same · detachment sells · price objections are value objections.

## 4. MY EXPLANATION RULE

(Exception: the Man brain, only when the persona is a man — see §3.) Features are explained through a personal, specific pain point or confession from lived experience; the product enters as the answer or evidence. Never lead with what the product does. Never announce the product with a feature list or an ingredient list. Demonstration is continuous — the product is in hand, being used, while the story is told — proof is shown as an artefact (a real moment, a real number, the thing visible in frame at the moment it's said), never described in vague language. Specific numbers over adjectives, always.

## 5. MY ENGAGEMENT STANDARD — the seven checkpoints, in order

Run every script through these after the spine (full standard in the companion skill):

1. **Hook with a withheld answer (0–3s)** — exact audience named, tension stated, answer withheld. Triple hook on.
2. **Payoff tease (3–8s)** — promise the payoff plainly without giving it away. Same beat as the shoulder tap (§1).
3. **Validation before the win (~25%)** — specific, true, earned; before any proof.
4. **Specific proof + midpoint re-hook (~50–60%)** — one specific, surprising, believable-scale proof point; a visual change on every new piece of proof, no static frame over 6 seconds, no noise cuts.
5. **Objection kill (right after proof)** — the single biggest dismissal, removed in one line.
6. **Payoff, then one CTA** — payoff delivered first; CTA set by account (soft for brand-attraction and personal brand, direct for brand deliverables); never both.
7. **The reframe (whole script)** — behind = advantage, stuck = missing system; no line shames the viewer.

## 6. MY HARD RULES

- **Never invent proof** — no invented results, numbers, timeframes, testimonials, claims, or scarcity. A missing proof point is marked **[NEED: the proof point]**, never filled in. Never borrow numbers, results, or scenes from another creator's video — their examples teach the shape, never the content.
- **No fake personal experience with any product, ever.** Never claim a result, symptom, or story she has not lived. (Integrity rule: no representing products or claims she has not lived and cannot stand behind.)
  **Actor-portrayal exception (ruling 13, 2026-11-16):** when the brand approves in writing an on-screen "Actor portrayal" (or "Dramatization") disclosure, she delivers the brand's own claims and first-person lines as written. The claims must come from the brand (their wording, their substantiation); the engine never invents them. The disclosure stays legible on screen while the claim plays, so the video is a dramatization, not a genuine testimonial. Health, treatment and before/after claims are still flagged to the brand as an ad-rejection risk to confirm. Still never, even with the disclosure: stating or implying she is an alcoholic or has a personal/family alcohol story, and her son holding or promoting supplements. Without the written approval, the integrity rule stands.
- **Only true urgency** — a real deadline, real stock, or the real cost of the problem continuing. Reject invented countdowns, stock numbers, and deadlines, and say they were rejected.
- **One CTA per video**, after the proof — soft follow invitation on brand-attraction and personal-brand accounts, direct (naming what the click gets them) on brand deliverables. Never both, never two.
- Specificity is a rule, not a style: replace every vague claim with a specific, true number, timeframe, or thing visible in frame — or delete the claim / mark it [NEED].
- Social proof is shown as an artefact — a real person's face and specific result, or a sourced count or rating on screen. "Everyone loves it" language is deleted.
- Word budget enforced by cutting words, never by telling her to talk faster.

## 7. MY VOICE

- Warm, candid, plain, specific. Spoken voice, FaceTime energy — like telling a friend. Full spoken sentences, teleprompter-ready (she always films from a teleprompter script, even on "natural" briefs); shot notes in [brackets] on their own lines.
- Concrete specifics from real life: real numbers, real timeframes, real moments. Systems language for ADHD content (loops, stuck points, "a system outside your head"). Personal-brand register: abundance and accountability — 1% better every day, get back up, keep going, you are loved exactly as you are.
- **Uses:** "Mahalo!" (sign-off in messages). Humor is real-life and creator-life — rates, revisions, filming chaos at home — self-aware, never polished-influencer.
- **Never:** empty praise words in hooks ("amazing," "obsessed," "game-changer"); apology language in follow-ups; invented urgency; telling a client they are her first inbound brand; framing herself publicly as "learning alongside" her audience; claiming any story she has not lived.
- Her record, the only proof numbers allowed: about 6 months in UGC, ~$15,000 made, ~$7,000 in one month, ~$2,000/month since, on 10–20 hours a week.

## DECISIONS ALAINA HAS MADE — carry these into every rebuild of this guide

Ruled 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16. These override any source document or companion skill that says otherwise.

1. **3–8s beat:** one beat, both jobs — shoulder tap (who it's for) + payoff tease, in one breath.
2. **Hooks:** the Engine's 4 patterns and the THIS/THAT 5 levers are merged into one list of seven (§2), used for writing AND rewriting.
3. **"I" openings:** the six lived-moment hooks (Survival Test, Emergency In Progress, Named Pain Killed, Missing Piece, Outcome Over Feature, One Thing I Changed) may open with "I". The Named Audience hook and all other copy never open with "I". (Replaces the earlier THIS/THAT-only exception.)
4. **Payoff timing (updated 2026-11-16, with Prompt 6):** sales scripts deliver the payoff as early as honestly possible (by ~20s), never withheld to the end, with proof after it. Growth and storytelling videos promise the outcome by ~20s and deliver the full payoff at the end, before the CTA.
5. **CTA:** set per account — soft for brand-attraction and personal brand, direct for brand deliverables. Never both.
6. **Spine:** the Script Engine spine (§1) is the only spine. The Creator Brief's Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA is retired everywhere.
7. **Persona + Man brain:** every script is written for the over-40 brain — always on. Still always ask who the persona is for the ad; the answer adds layers on top of the 40s brain, never replaces it. The Man brain (specs and price up front) applies only when the persona is a man, and then overrides the lived-moment rule for that video.
8. **Hook length:** under 15 words is a hard limit; longer lines get split, with the overflow moving into the 3–8s beat.
9. **Persona questions + never sound like an ad:** every project asks the four persona questions (§3) — who it's for, her pain points, how the product solves each, and the emotional outcome. Outcomes are shown as feelings, never features. Always selling, never sounding like an ad.
10. **Show door:** dropped on sales scripts (research: curiosity is the weakest sales angle, 0.71×). Sales campaigns rotate Feeling, Fix, Joke, Close. The Show door stays available for growth/non-sales videos. (2026-11-16)
11. **Demos:** the demo is a shot inside the video, never the whole format — including screen-recording app demos. Julia's "teach exactly how" lives in those demo beats inside a talking-head, voiceover B-roll, or review frame. (2026-11-16)
12. **Cut pace:** cut on new information, never for motion. New information beat every 7–10s, no static frame over 6s, no noise cuts. The Script Engine's 3–5s shot rule survives only as a guide for proof sections where each shot shows something new. Per the research grades, the second counts are SOFT defaults; "every change carries information" is the firm rule. (2026-11-16)
13. **Actor portrayal:** with the brand's written approval and an on-screen "Actor portrayal" disclosure, she delivers the brand's own claims and first-person lines as written; health/treatment/before-after claims are still flagged as ad-rejection risks for the brand to confirm; the alcohol-story and son-with-supplements lines stay off-limits. Without that approval, the integrity rule stands. (2026-11-16)

## Original contradictions as flagged (kept for the record — all resolved above)

1. **The 3–8s beat has three names.** Script Engine calls it THE PROMISE (tease the payoff). The Engagement Standard calls it PAYOFF TEASE. Lana's shoulder tap — adopted into the engine prompt pack — says the same slot names who the video is for (identity / problem / situation). This guide layers them (tap = how the promise is delivered), but Alaina has not formally ruled that the shoulder tap replaces the promise beat. **Decide:** one beat doing both jobs, or two beats?
2. **Two hook taxonomies.** The Script Engine lists 4 hook patterns (Survival Test, Emergency in Progress, One Thing I Changed, Plot Twist). The THIS/THAT skill lists 5 levers (Survival Test, Emergency In Progress, Named Pain Killed, Missing Piece, Outcome Over Feature). "One Thing I Changed" and "Plot Twist" have no lever equivalent; "Named Pain Killed" and "Outcome Over Feature" have no Engine-pattern equivalent ("Missing Piece" ≈ "Plot Twist" in shape, not in wording). **Decide:** merge into one list, or keep Engine patterns for writing and THAT levers for rewriting (the THIS/THAT doc's own framing)?
3. **Payoff timing.** Julia's rule sells the outcome inside ~20 seconds; the Script Engine withholds the payoff to the end (teasing at 3–8s, delivering at the close). Julia's own skill reconciles this as "the ~20-second payoff rule lives inside the tease and proof beats" — promise early, delivery at the end — but the plain readings conflict. **Decide:** confirm the reconciliation stands as written here.
4. **CTA wording.** The Script Engine's CTA rules are all direct ("name what the click GETS them"); the Engagement Standard adds the soft CTA for growth content and says the Engine's rule still governs offers. This guide follows the Standard's split (soft = growth, direct = offers). **Decide:** confirm, since the Engine doc alone would ban the soft CTA.
5. **Two spines.** The Creator Intelligence Brief calls Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA "non-negotiable"; this guide uses the Script Engine spine.
6. **Man brain vs. the explanation rule.** Sheila's Man brain leads with specs and price; the explanation rule says never lead with what the product does.
7. **Hook length.** THIS/THAT says under 15 words; its own 47-open-tabs example runs ~27 words.
