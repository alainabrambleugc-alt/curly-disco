# UGC creative strategy playbook

Reference for the teardown engine. The creator profile overrides anything here.

## 1. The reasoning lens

- **Buyer first.** People buy the life outcome, not the feature. Frame every line from what emotionally and psychologically connects for this persona, and keep curiosity open so they stay to the end.
- **Brand second, always.** Meta and TikTok read the video to decide who sees it, so the creative does the targeting. Most test ads lose and winners wear out. Fresh angles and formats find winners faster and save ad spend. The creator who saves them money is the one they come back to.
- **Never about the creator's skills** in isolation. Translate every skill into the brand's goal: product recognition, more sales, a new product launch in a similar line, a promotion, or a guarantee they now offer.

## 2. Persona identification (answer at the top of every brief)

1. Who exactly are we speaking to? Give them a name, age and one-line life sketch.
2. Age group and life stage (new mom, empty nester, retiree, college student, busy professional…)
3. The pain point, in their own words: what they'd say to a friend, not marketing speak.
4. What they've already tried, and why it let them down.
5. What's stopping them buying: price, skepticism, effort, "I've tried everything", switching cost, fear of looking silly.
6. What they secretly want, and what they're afraid of.
7. Awareness stage (section 4), and what the first 2 seconds must do because of it.

### Benefit ladder

Climb from the feature to the life outcome, and lead with the top rung:

| Feature | What it does | What that means for their life |
|---|---|---|
| Turmeric supplement | Lowers blood pressure | I'll be around longer for my kids and grandkids |
| 20–30 mmHg compression socks | Better circulation | I can stand through a 12-hour shift and still play with my kids after |
| 15% vitamin C serum | Brighter-looking skin | I feel confident going out without makeup |

The feature is proof; the life outcome is the hook and the emotional payoff.

## 3. Marketing psychology toolkit

Pick one primary trigger per concept and name it.

- **Curiosity gap / open loop:** promise an answer, delay it ("the third one surprised me"). Pay it off before the CTA.
- **Loss aversion:** what they keep losing without it (time, money, sleep, moments with family) hits harder than what they'd gain.
- **Identity:** "for women who…", "if you're the friend who…". People buy what fits who they are.
- **Social proof:** real numbers, sold-out moments, reviews (only real ones from the brand).
- **Specificity:** "$12, 30 seconds, every morning" beats "affordable and fast".
- **Pattern interrupt:** an unexpected action, object or line that breaks the scroll.
- **Future pacing:** let them picture life after ("imagine your Saturday when…").
- **Authority / mechanism:** why it works, the one ingredient or design detail.
- **Contrast:** old way vs new way, without vs with.
- **Reciprocity:** give a tip of real value first, then the product.
- **Urgency / scarcity:** only real ones (code expiry, seasonal stock).

## 4. Awareness stages and funnel

| Stage | Funnel | The customer… | The opening should… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Top (TOF) | Doesn't know they have the problem | Lead with a relatable moment or identity |
| Problem-aware | Top (TOF) | Has the problem, doesn't know a solution exists | Name the pain sharply, reveal there's a fix |
| Solution-aware | Middle (MOF) | Knows solutions exist, needs to know why THIS one is better | Lead with the differentiator |
| Product-aware | Middle/Bottom | Knows this product, hasn't bought | Kill the #1 objection; proof, comparison, reviews |
| Most aware | Bottom (BOF) | Ready, needs a reason now | Offer, code, guarantee, urgency |

### Differentiators for solution-aware buyers

When they know solutions exist and need a reason to pick this one, lead with whichever the brief supports:
- The ingredients, or one hero ingredient nobody else has
- Ease of use
- Guarantee / risk reversal
- Where it's sourced or made (e.g. made in the USA vs imported)
- Cost or cost per use
- Results timeline (brand-approved only)
- Sustainability, values, founder story

## 5. Persuasion angles (each concept uses a different one)

Pain / agitation · Transformation / aspiration · Social proof · Authority / mechanism · Objection-buster · Value / comparison · Convenience / time · Identity · Curiosity / contrarian · Urgency / offer

## 6. Formats (each concept uses a different one)

| Format | Core move | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonial | Experience to camera | Experience claims need actor portrayal or a spokesperson read |
| Demo | Show it working, hands-on | The result must be visible; cut every pause |
| Listicle | "3 reasons…", numbered beats | Each point tied to a life benefit |
| Montage | Fast cuts of use moments + VO/text | Needs one clear through-line |
| Split screen | Old way vs new, with vs without | Honest; no staged fake "before" |
| BTS | Unfiltered real life / process | Still needs a hook and a product moment |
| Tutorial | Step-by-step use | Lead with the result, then the steps |
| Unboxing | Reveal and reaction | Add a demo beat after the reveal |

## 7. Hooks: always three layers

Every hook has all three, working together:
- **Visual hook:** what the eye sees in frame one, already moving.
- **Spoken hook:** under ~3 seconds; no "Hey guys" or "So…".
- **On-screen text hook:** 3–7 words, readable sound-off, adds to the spoken line rather than repeating it word for word.

Hook types: callout · pain · curiosity gap · contrarian · result-first · social proof · confession · question (only if the honest answer is "yes") · pattern interrupt.

### Motion in the first 2 seconds (mandatory)

Never open on a static talking head. Options:
- Walk into frame, or lean into the lens
- Push the product toward the lens, or put something in front of the camera then reveal
- Take off sunglasses, flip your hair, turn around to camera
- Drop, pour, rip, snap, spray, stretch the product
- Hand enters frame holding the product
- Whip pan or quick reframe

Opening with the product in hand before the intro buys an extra second of attention.

## 8. Retention: director's rules

- **Movement every 5–7 seconds:** an angle change (wide → standard → macro), a gesture, a product action, a walk, a prop swap, or a B-roll insert. Static shots longer than 7 seconds bleed viewers.
- **Re-hook** just before attention dips (~8–12s in a 30s video, and again around the midpoint of longer ones): a new open loop or surprise. "But that's not even the best part." "Here's what nobody tells you." "Watch what happens when…"
- **Open loops:** tease the payoff early and deliver it before the CTA.
- **On-screen text changes** with each beat, giving the eye something new.
- **Pacing:** cut breaths and pauses. ~2.5 spoken words per second: 15s ≈ 35 words · 30s ≈ 70 · 45s ≈ 110 · 60s ≈ 145.
- **Payoff:** the end delivers what the hook promised, then the CTA follows straight away.
- **B-roll vs in-camera movement:** the base rate doesn't include B-roll. Hit the rhythm with in-camera angle changes and product motion by default; mark B-roll inserts as optional upgrades.

### Three angles for every concept

| Angle | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Wide** | Context, lifestyle, walking in, the setting (great for the off-grid look) |
| **Standard (chest-up / medium)** | Talking to camera, the main delivery |
| **Close-up macro** | Texture, ingredients, the product mechanism, details only a real user notices |

Cutting between them creates movement without B-roll.

## 9. Product motion and framing

For each demo beat, the script says **what the product is doing while the line is spoken**, and how it's framed:
- *"Say 'look how wide this band is' while stretching the top band of the compression sock in frame."*
- *"Pump the serum onto two fingers on 'one pump is all you need', in macro."*

**Safe zones (9:16):**
- **Top ~15%:** keep clear above your head for the text overlay (and the platform's top tabs).
- **Bottom ~25%:** keep clear for captions, the caption/sound bar and the CTA button.
- **Right ~15%:** the like, comment and share buttons sit here.
- **Demo action sits in the middle third**, never under the text or captions.
- Leave headroom when framing yourself so the overlay doesn't sit on your face.

## 10. CTAs that get the click

One action + a reason to do it now + a friction remover.
- "Tap the link and use GLOW20 before it ends Dec 31. It's 20% off and fragrance-free, so nothing to lose."
- "Grab it from the orange cart below while the bundle's still in stock."
Match the CTA to the funnel: TOF = soft ("see why everyone's switching"), BOF = hard (code, guarantee, deadline). For paid ads, keep a 2–3 second CTA that can be cut on.

## 11. Platform specs (when the brief is silent)

| Platform | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | 9:16, 1080×1920 | Safe zones above |
| Meta feed ads | 4:5 or 1:1 | Shoot 9:16 with room to crop |
| Paid ads | — | Hook and CTA as separable clips; licensed music only |

## 12. Compliance

- **Experience claims:** first-person experience she hasn't had is delivered only with an on-screen "Actor portrayal" / "Dramatization" disclosure approved by the brand in writing, legible and on screen while the claim plays. Otherwise use a spokesperson read. Claims come from the brand (their wording and substantiation), never invented.
- Disease or cure claims: flag as an ad-rejection and regulatory risk for the brand to confirm, even with a disclosure.
- Disclose the paid partnership (#ad / paid-partnership label) unless the brand handles it.
- Before/after only if genuine and approved; no competitors shown or named unless allowed.

## 13. Pushing back on briefs

Push back with commercial reasoning: it moves her from interchangeable vendor to strategic partner. Always name the brand's goal and show how the change gets them more of it.

| Trigger | Move |
|---|---|
| Experience claims | Offer the actor-portrayal disclosure or a spokesperson read; it protects their ad account too |
| Monotone formats (every video the same concept) | Recommend format variations so they have contrasting creative mechanics to test |
| Scope over budget | Two clear options: the full brief at standard rate, or a trimmed package at their figure |

Tone model: "Happy to film this. If we open on the product in hand before the intro, we buy an extra second of viewer attention. I can shoot both versions so your team can test them."

## 14. Upsell and add-on menu

Always offer packages plus 2–4 add-ons picked to fit the brief:
- **Extra hooks / CTAs** to test openings and closes on the same body
- **Different lengths** (15s / 30s / 60s cuts) to see what holds viewers
- **Funnel versions:** TOF (problem-aware, emotional), MOF (solution-aware, differentiator-led), BOF (offer, guarantee, urgency)
- **Awareness versions:** "they have the problem and don't know the fix" vs "they know the fix and need to know why this one is better" (lead with the differentiator)
- **Different angles and formats:** a bigger test batch across multiple formats
- **Strategy review:** a look at their analytics or past ads to shape the next round (`[quote]` unless priced in the rate card)
- **Raw footage, platform reformats, B-roll, rush**
- **Retainer:** turn the winners into a monthly content plan

Also mention: any special or sale she's running, platform coupons, and "I already own it, so no sample needed" (saves them product and shipping).

## 15. Brand communication (offers, pitches, follow-ups, check-ins)

- Open with yes or with their goal, not with yourself.
- Frame everything as: buyer's emotional outcome → how this content gets the brand its goal (recognition, sales, launch, promo, guarantee).
- Skills appear only as the means to their result ("I'll open on the product in motion so the ad holds attention past the first second").
- One suggestion, one reason tied to their results, one easy next step.
- Warm, direct, conversational. Under six sentences for replies. No em dashes, jargon or grovelling.

**Pitch:** their goal or a gap you spotted in their ads → the concept that fills it → the result it's built for → a simple next step.
**Follow-up:** one new piece of value (an angle idea, a trend in their niche), then a light ask.
**Post-deliverable check-in:** ask how the content is performing against their goal, offer a next round built on what's winning (new hooks on the winner, a new funnel stage, a retainer).
