# UGC craft playbook

Reference for choosing angles, writing hooks and structuring scripts. The creator profile overrides anything here.

## Angle / format library

| Format | Best for | Core move | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem → solution | Products that fix a clear pain | Show the pain fast, product as the turn | Pain must be real and specific, not generic |
| Routine / "get ready with me" | Beauty, wellness, daily-use products | Product woven into a natural routine | Product can get lost — give it a hero moment |
| Honest review / "is it worth it" | Higher price, skeptical audience | Lead with a doubt, resolve with proof | Must still land positive; don't over-hedge |
| Before / after | Visible results (cleaning, skin, hair, home) | Contrast cut | Claim risk — needs brief approval; no edited "after" |
| Unboxing / first impressions | Launches, packaging, gifting | Genuine reaction | Weak without a demo beat after the reveal |
| Demo / how-to | Products with a "how" or a trick | Hands-on, step by step | Keep it moving — cut every pause |
| Comparison / "I switched from" | Category with a known default | Old way vs new way | Never name a competitor unless the brief allows it |
| Storytime | Emotional or lifestyle products | Personal story, product as the turning point | Get to the product before ~40% of runtime |
| Myth-busting / "stop doing X" | Educational niches | Contrarian opener, product as the right way | Don't make claims the brief doesn't support |
| Listicle / "3 things I…" | Multi-feature products | Numbered beats keep retention | Feature dump — tie each to a benefit |
| Day in the life | Lifestyle, apps, food, fitness | Product shows up in real moments | Slow pacing; front-load the hook |
| Reply-to-comment / FAQ | Objection handling | Answer a (realistic) question on screen | Comment must be plausible, not a fake claim |
| Street-style / POV skit | Playful brands | Relatable scenario, text-driven | Brand tone must allow humour |

## Hook types

- **Callout:** names the viewer — "If you have curly hair and live somewhere humid…"
- **Pain / frustration:** "I was so tired of my coffee going cold by 9am."
- **Curiosity gap:** "This $12 thing replaced three products in my bathroom."
- **Contrarian:** "Stop buying expensive serums. Here's why."
- **Result-first:** show the end state in frame one, then rewind.
- **Social proof:** "This sold out three times and I finally get why."
- **Confession / storytime:** "I didn't want to like this."
- **Visual pattern-interrupt:** unexpected action or object in the first frame (pouring, ripping, dropping, a hand into frame).
- **Question:** only if the viewer's honest answer is "yes" — "Do your feet hurt after a shift?"

Rules: under ~3s spoken; the first frame must already be moving; on-screen text restates the hook in 3–7 words; no "Hey guys" or "So…" lead-ins.

## Script beats

1. **Hook (0–3s)** — stop the scroll.
2. **Problem / tension (3–8s)** — make it specific and felt.
3. **Product intro (by ~8–10s)** — name it, show it in hand.
4. **Proof / demo** — texture, use, result, a detail only someone who used it would notice.
5. **CTA** — one action, matching the brief (code, link, "tap the shop button"). For paid ads, give a 2–3s CTA that can be cut on.

Pacing: ~2.5 spoken words per second. 15s ≈ 35 words · 30s ≈ 70 · 45s ≈ 110 · 60s ≈ 145.

## Platform specs (defaults when the brief is silent)

| Platform | Ratio | Safe zone notes |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | 9:16, 1080×1920 | Keep text out of the bottom ~20% and the right ~15% (UI buttons) |
| Meta feed ads | 4:5 or 1:1 | Shoot 9:16 with room to crop |
| Paid ads generally | — | Provide hook and CTA as separable clips; avoid music with copyright |

## Compliance quick list

- Disclose paid partnerships (#ad / platform's paid-partnership label) unless the brief says the brand handles it.
- No health, medical, weight-loss or income claims beyond the brief's exact approved wording.
- Before/after: genuine, unedited, and only if the brief approves.
- Don't show or name competitors unless the brief allows it.
- Use only licensed or platform-provided music for paid usage.
