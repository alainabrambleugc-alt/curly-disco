# UGC strategy playbook (teardown add-ons)

**Order of authority:** `storytelling-guide.md` (and its "Decisions Alaina has made") → `creator-profile.md` → `research-notes.md` → this file. This file covers only what the guide doesn't: brand strategy, funnel and awareness, angle and format choice, the three camera angles, product motion and framing, pushback, upsells and brand communication. For the spine, hooks, brains and doors, persona questions, explanation rule, Engagement Standard, voice and hard rules, use the storytelling guide. Never restate them differently here.

## 1. Why brands care (the commercial lens)

- Meta and TikTok read the video itself to decide who sees it, so the creative does much of the targeting.
- Testing reality: high fatigue, low win rates. Most test ads lose, and winners wear out.
- Fresh angles and formats find a winner faster and save ad spend. The creator who saves them money is the one they come back to.
- Judge UGC on sales, not clicks: creator-style creative loses on CTR but wins on conversion and ROAS (research-notes). Use that framing with brands.
- Never pitch her skills for their own sake. Translate every skill into the brand's goal: product recognition, more sales, a new product in a similar line, a promotion or deal, or a guarantee they now offer.

## 2. Benefit ladder (worked examples)

Climb from feature to lived outcome; the ad leads with the top rung, shown as a feeling (storytelling guide §3–4).

| Feature | What it does | What she FEELS once it's solved |
|---|---|---|
| Turmeric supplement | Lowers blood pressure | More years with my kids and grandkids (brand-approved claim only) |
| 20–30 mmHg compression socks | Better circulation | I get through a 12-hour shift and still have legs for my kid after |
| 15% vitamin C serum | Brighter-looking skin | I leave the house without reaching for concealer first |

## 3. Awareness stages and funnel

| Stage | Funnel | The buyer… | Opening job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | TOF | Doesn't know she has the problem | A lived moment she recognizes |
| Problem-aware | TOF | Has the problem, no known fix | Name the pain in her words, signal a fix exists |
| Solution-aware | MOF | Knows fixes exist, needs why THIS one | Lead with the differentiator |
| Product-aware | MOF/BOF | Knows the product, hasn't bought | Kill the one objection with visible proof |
| Most aware | BOF | Ready, needs a reason now | The deal, guarantee or true deadline |

**Differentiators** for solution-aware buyers (only what the brief supports): the hero ingredient nobody else has · ease of use · guarantee / risk reversal · where it's sourced or made (e.g. made in the USA) · cost or cost per use · brand-approved results timeline · values or founder story.

## 4. Choosing angles (research-tuned)

On sales videos, prefer the angles that sell per view (research-notes, MediaLabs): **true urgency/scarcity 1.35× · value/deal 1.26× · social proof 1.21×**. Problem→solution is the structure, not the pitch (0.98×). **Curiosity is the weakest sales angle (0.71×)**: it earns watches, not sales. Use it for growth videos, not as the selling angle.

Other angles that pair well with her doors: identity/belonging (Fogg) · objection-buster · convenience/time (Mom brain) · authority shown by demonstration · transformation shown as a feeling.

Each of the four concepts uses a **different door** (sales: Feeling, Fix, Joke, Close; no Show door) **and a different angle and format**.

## 5. Formats

| Format | Per-view signal (research) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Voiceover B-roll (incl. walk-and-talk) | 1.24× | Her strongest sales format by the data |
| Unboxing | 1.20× | Add the product-in-use shot after the reveal |
| Testimonial / review | 1.17× | Only her lived experience; no fake testimonial, ever |
| Talking head | 1.10× | Triple hook; product in hand |
| Montage | — | Needs one through-line |
| Listicle | — | Each point lands as an outcome, not a feature |
| Split screen | — | Honest contrast; no staged "before" |
| BTS | — | Her real homestead life; still needs a product moment |
| Demo / tutorial | 0.96× as a whole format | **A shot inside another format, never the whole format** (her ruling 11), screen-recording app demos included |

## 6. Shots: three angles per concept

| Angle | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Wide** | Context and place: the Quonset hut, lanai, garden, rainforest; walking into frame |
| **Standard (chest-up)** | Teleprompter delivery to camera |
| **Close-up macro** | Texture, ingredient, mechanism, hands using the product |

Cut between them **on new information, never for motion** (her ruling 12): a meaningful visual change on every new thought, a new information beat about every 7–10s, no static frame over 6s, no noise cuts. Those second counts are soft defaults; "every change carries information" is the firm rule.

**Frame 1** (research, STRONG): face + product + on-screen text all present in the first second; a product cue inside 2 seconds, shown visually while the spoken opener is still the lived moment. Open with motion: walking into frame, lifting the product into shot, sunglasses off, hands already busy.

## 7. Product motion and framing

Every demo beat says **what the product is doing while the line is spoken** and how it's framed, e.g.:
- *[Say "look how wide this band is" while stretching the top band of the compression sock in frame. Macro, band in the middle of the frame.]*
- *[Pump the serum onto two fingers on "one pump," macro, product label legible.]*

**Safe zones (9:16, research STRONG):** keep hook text, prices and CTA clear of the **top 14%**, the **bottom 35%** and **6% at each side**. Leave headroom so the text overlay sits above her head, not on her face; keep the demo action in the middle band, never under text or captions.

**Captions:** phrase-level cards of 3–7 words that carry the meaning with the sound off. No karaoke word-highlighting.

## 8. Claims

Her integrity rule governs (storytelling guide §6, creator profile §3 and §6): never claim a result, symptom or story she hasn't lived; missing proof is `[NEED: the proof point]`. **Exception (ruling 13):** with the brand's written approval and a legible on-screen "Actor portrayal" disclosure, she delivers the brand's own claims and first-person lines as written. The brand supplies and substantiates the claims; health/treatment/before-after claims are still flagged as ad-rejection risks; the alcohol-story and son-with-supplements lines stay off-limits. Health and supplement claims beyond the brand's exact approved wording, treatment or insomnia claims, prescription comparisons, specific-results promises and before/after framing are flagged. She only uses or applies a product after a paid project with a signed contract, so she has days with it, not weeks: timeframe claims go through the actor-portrayal path or become a spokesperson read or [NEED].

## 9. Pushing back on briefs

Push back with commercial reasoning: it moves her from interchangeable vendor to strategic partner. Name the brand's goal and show how the change gets them more of it.

| Trigger | Move |
|---|---|
| Claims she hasn't lived | Offer the on-screen "Actor portrayal" disclosure, or a spokesperson read in their approved wording; either protects their ad account |
| Monotone formats | Different doors and formats so they have contrasting creative mechanics to test |
| Scope over budget | Two clear options: the full brief at her rate, or a trimmed package at their figure |
| Unscripted / freestyle demand | She'll keep the natural feel and send a storyboard for approval first (workflow note, not a dealbreaker) |
| Profile hard nos (§6) | Decline that part plainly and offer the paid alternative |

Tone model: "Happy to film this. If we open on the product in hand before the intro, we buy an extra second of viewer attention. I can shoot both versions so your team can test them."

## 10. Upsells and add-ons

Always offer packages plus 2–4 add-ons that fit the brief:
- Extra hooks / CTA alternatives on the same body (her "one product, five hooks" rotation)
- Different lengths (15 / 30 / 60s)
- Funnel versions: TOF (lived pain), MOF (differentiator), BOF (deal, guarantee, true deadline)
- Awareness versions: "has the problem, doesn't know the fix" vs "knows the fix, needs why this one" (lead with the differentiator)
- A door rotation across a campaign (Feeling, Fix, Joke, Close)
- A bigger test batch across formats
- Strategy review of their analytics or past ads (price per rate card)
- Raw footage, platform reformats, B-roll, rush
- Retainer: turn the winners into a monthly content plan

Also mention any real, dated special she's running, platform coupons, and "I already own it, so no sample needed."

## 11. Brand communication (offers, pitches, follow-ups, check-ins)

Voice rules come from the storytelling guide §7 and creator profile §3. In short:
- **Never open with "I"** or ego-forward framing. Open with their goal, their buyer, or a yes.
- Buyer's emotional outcome → how the content gets the brand its goal. Skills appear only as the means.
- One suggestion, one reason tied to their results, one easy next step. Warm, plain, specific. Under six sentences for replies.
- No em dashes. No apology language. No invented urgency (only a real, dated special). Never say they're her first inbound brand. Never frame her as a beginner or "learning alongside."
- Proof numbers: only her real record (creator profile §1).
- Sign off "Mahalo!" in messages. Trybe messages: no em dashes, 1,000 characters max.

**Pitch:** their goal or a gap in their ads → the concept that fills it → the result it's built for → one easy next step.
**Follow-up:** one new piece of value (an angle, a trend in their niche), then a light ask. No apology.
**Post-deliverable check-in:** how is it performing against their goal → a next round built on what's winning (new hooks on the winner, a new funnel stage, a retainer).
