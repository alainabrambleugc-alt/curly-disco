# UGC creative strategy playbook

Reference for the teardown engine. The creator profile overrides anything here.

## Why brands care (the reasoning lens)

- Meta and TikTok read the video itself to decide who should see it. The creative does much of the targeting.
- Testing reality: high fatigue, low win rates. Most test ads lose, and winners wear out.
- Fresh angles and formats help the brand find a winner faster, which saves ad spend. The creator who saves them money is the one they come back to.
- So: recommend variety (different angles × different formats), and always explain suggestions in terms of the brand's results.

## Awareness stages

| Stage | The customer… | The opening should… |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know they have the problem | Lead with a relatable moment or identity, not the product |
| Problem-aware | Feels the pain, doesn't know solutions | Name the pain sharply, then reveal there's a fix |
| Solution-aware | Knows solutions exist, not this one | Show why this one is different (mechanism, demo) |
| Product-aware | Knows this product, hasn't bought | Kill the main objection; proof, comparison, reviews |
| Most aware | Ready, needs a reason now | Offer, code, urgency, straight to the point |

Infer the stage from the brief's audience, offer and whether the ads are cold-traffic or retargeting. Cold paid traffic is usually problem- or solution-aware.

## Persuasion angles

Each of the four concepts must use a different one.

- **Pain / agitation:** make the problem felt, product as relief
- **Transformation / aspiration:** the life after, not the product
- **Social proof:** "everyone's switching", reviews, sold-out moments (only real ones)
- **Authority / mechanism:** why it works, the ingredient or design detail
- **Objection-buster:** take on the #1 reason they don't buy, head on
- **Value / comparison:** cost per use, replaces X things, vs the old way (no named competitors unless allowed)
- **Convenience / time:** how fast or easy it is
- **Identity:** "for people who…", made for someone like me
- **Curiosity / contrarian:** "stop doing X", the thing nobody tells you
- **Urgency / offer:** limited-time code, seasonal moment

## Formats

Each of the four concepts must use a different one.

| Format | Core move | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonial | Real experience, talking to camera | Only claim what you've actually experienced |
| Demo | Show it working, hands-on | Every pause cut; the result must be visible |
| Listicle | "3 reasons…", numbered beats | Tie each point to a benefit, not a feature dump |
| Montage | Fast cuts of use moments + voiceover/text | Needs a clear through-line message |
| Split screen | Side-by-side: old way vs new, with vs without | Keep it honest; no staged fake "before" |
| BTS | Behind the scenes: unfiltered, process, "real life" | Still needs a hook and a product moment |
| Tutorial | Step-by-step how to use it | Lead with the result, then the steps |
| Unboxing | First reveal and reaction | Weak without a demo beat after the reveal |

## Hook types

- **Callout:** "If you have curly hair and live somewhere humid…"
- **Pain:** "I was so tired of my coffee going cold by 9am."
- **Curiosity gap:** "This $12 thing replaced three products in my bathroom."
- **Contrarian:** "Stop buying expensive serums."
- **Result-first:** show the end state in frame one.
- **Social proof:** "This sold out three times and I finally get why."
- **Confession:** "I didn't want to like this."
- **Visual pattern-interrupt:** pouring, ripping, a hand into frame, product already in hand.

Rules: under ~3s spoken; frame one already moving; on-screen text restates the hook in 3–7 words; no "Hey guys" or "So…". Opening on the product in hand before the intro buys an extra second of attention.

## Script beats

1. **Hook (0–3s)**
2. **Problem / tension (3–8s)** — specific and felt
3. **Product (by ~8–10s)** — named and in hand
4. **Proof / demo** — use, texture, result, a detail only a real user would notice
5. **CTA** — one action. For paid ads, a 2–3s separable CTA.

Pace ~2.5 spoken words/sec: 15s ≈ 35 words · 30s ≈ 70 · 45s ≈ 110 · 60s ≈ 145.

## Platform specs (when the brief is silent)

| Platform | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | 9:16, 1080×1920 | Keep text out of the bottom ~20% and right ~15% |
| Meta feed ads | 4:5 or 1:1 | Shoot 9:16 with room to crop |
| Paid ads | — | Hook and CTA as separable clips; licensed music only |

## Compliance

- **Experience claims:** if a script asks you to claim an experience you haven't had (a timeframe, a result, a problem you don't have), pivot to a spokesperson read. Faking a review violates FTC endorsement guidelines and exposes the creator to liability.
- Disclose paid partnerships (#ad / paid-partnership label) unless the brand handles it.
- No health, medical, weight-loss or income claims beyond the brief's exact approved wording.
- Before/after only if genuine, unedited and approved.
- No competitors shown or named unless allowed.

## Pushing back on briefs

Pushing back with commercial reasoning shows you care about ad performance, which moves you from interchangeable vendor to strategic partner.

| Trigger | Move |
|---|---|
| Unethical claims (experience you never had) | Pivot to a spokesperson read; explain it protects them too |
| Monotone formats (every video the same concept) | Recommend format variations so they have contrasting creative mechanics to test |
| Scope mismatch (scope exceeds stated budget) | Two clear options: the complete brief at standard rate, or a trimmed package at their figure |

Tone model: "Happy to film this. If we open on the product in hand before the intro, we buy an extra second of viewer attention. I can shoot both versions so your team can test them."

## Client message style

- Start with yes ("Happy to film this", "Love this brief").
- One suggestion, one reason tied to their results, one easy next step.
- Warm, direct, under six sentences. No em dashes. Never sounds like you're rejecting their idea.
- Offers: two clear options, conversational, focused on what they get. Close by noting that once you see what performed, you can turn the winners into a monthly content plan.
